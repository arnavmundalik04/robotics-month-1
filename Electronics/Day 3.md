Day 3

Capacitor: Stores electrical energy. Can discharge it faster than a battery can. Used to smoothen the output supply. 
Two values represented on the capacitor are the capacitance measure in farads and the voltage which is the maximum voltage the capacitor can handle. These are used for power factor correction and to smoothen out peaks when converting AC to DC power. 

Diodes: These allow the current to flow only in one direction. Going into the basics of conductor, insulator and semiconductor. Conductors have free electrons in the valence shell of an atom. Hence, they conduct electricity well because electrons can jump around frequently. Opposite is the case for an insulator. Semiconductors can behave both as a conductor and as a insulator. Materials like silicon which have no free electrons can be doped to be P type and N type. Combining them we get diodes. P and N type regions when put together cause a PN junction. 
Diodes can be used to convert AC to DC. 

LED’s: These are light emitting diodes. These usually have a forward voltage that needs to be overcome to get the LED to glow. It is also important to use resistors with LEDs to limit the voltage going into them since they can blow up if excess voltage flows through them. 

Transistors: These are important devices that can act as a switch to turn things on or off and also amplify signals. These have three terminals,
1.	Base: Used to control the transistor. A small amount of base current allows for a larger current to flow from collector to emitter. Base = Switch ON or OFF. 
2.	Collector: This is where the current enters and the collector connects to the device you want to control. 
3.	Emitter: This is normally where the current exits. The Emitter is usually connected to the GND. 
There are resin head transistors that are low power ones that don’t get too hot and then there are metal head transistors that are attached to heat sinks to dissipate hate since they are used in high power applications. These have three pins namely emitter, base and collector. These also have a buffer voltage to start operating. 
Typically, there is a very small base current, and the collector has a large current. Thus, a small base-current can be used to control the large collector current acting as a amplifier. 
Pull up and Pull down resistors: Pull up resistors are used to pull up the voltage to VCC and pull-down resistors are used to pull down the voltage to ground or GND.
